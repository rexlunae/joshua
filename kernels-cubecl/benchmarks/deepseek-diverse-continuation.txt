Use one read-only mapping for the quantized weights and share it across sessions. The GPU budget must include dense weights, the active KV caches, temporary workspaces, and a bounded expert cache. Reserve headroom before admitting another request. For example, a 16 GiB device might dedicate roughly 9 GiB to dense weights, leaving several GiB for caches and scratch buffers, but the exact split must come from measurements.

Prefill several requests in short chunks so a long prompt cannot block every decode step. Maintain a queue of ready sessions, launch their token work in a predictable order, and preserve masks and positions across sliding-window wraparound. Cache continuation must produce the same logits as an uninterrupted run. Keep routing IDs and intermediate activations on the accelerator where possible. Avoid copying complete expert pools or rebuilding concatenated KV caches on every token.

Track time to first token, median decode latency, tail latency, and total throughput separately. A faster matrix-vector kernel may help decode much more than prefill. Count host transfers and synchronization points, measure private host memory including swapped pages, and distinguish file-backed mapped pages from owned allocations. Repeat sessions to detect retained scratch buffers. Test different expert routes, repeated tokens, long contexts, batched prompts, and cache boundaries before changing the production defaults.
